# Resume content strategy

Create a selective, factually grounded master resume that gives later tailoring work a reliable source of truth.

## Start with evidence

Before drafting, inventory the candidate's roles, dates, education, projects, tools, scope, outcomes, and links. Keep unknown facts explicitly unknown. Never invent metrics, titles, employers, credentials, technologies, work authorization, or outcomes. Ask only for missing details that materially affect a claim.

Separate source facts from proposed wording. When a metric is unavailable, prefer a specific, verifiable description of scope, frequency, scale, method, or user impact over a vague or fabricated number.

For a concise evidence, structure, and privacy checklist, read [the content reference](references/resume-evidence.md).

## Select and write

- Establish the target role, seniority, geography, and page-length constraint before deciding what to foreground.
- Prioritize recent, relevant evidence; retain older experience only when it differentiates the candidate or closes an important requirement.
- Use accomplishment bullets that identify the action, object, method or constraint, and demonstrated result. Do not force every bullet into an artificial formula.
- Make skills concrete and defensible. Group them only when grouping improves scanning; omit tools the candidate cannot discuss.
- Keep tense, dates, capitalization, role naming, punctuation, and chronology internally consistent.
- Treat a resume as an accessible professional document: plain section labels and standard role/company/date information should remain visible even if design is minimal.

## Deliverable

Return a master-content outline and draft copy, distinguishing verified facts, candidate-confirmed wording, and questions. Do not edit the user's Overleaf files unless the request includes that work; use `$overleaf-resume-authoring` for it.
