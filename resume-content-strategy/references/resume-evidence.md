# Resume evidence reference

Use this reference while forming a master resume or deciding whether an entry belongs in a resume or an academic CV.

## Evidence and presentation

- Resume language should be specific, active, factual, concise, and easy to scan. Quantify or qualify outcomes when the candidate can substantiate them.
- Put the most relevant information first and use reverse chronological ordering within experience sections unless a legitimate target-specific structure is clearer.
- Preserve ordinary, readable headings and role/company/date information. Basic formatting and explicit skills help both human readers and applicant-tracking systems.
- Do not add a photo, age, gender, references, or private/proprietary information unless the candidate specifically needs them for the applicable local context and has authorized disclosure.
- A CV is not simply a long resume: academic and research applications may require publications, presentations, teaching, grants, or other categories with different length expectations.
